Ada Lovelace

The world's first computer programmer

Biography

Augusta Ada King, Countess of Lovelace, was an English mathematician and writer born in 1815. The daughter of poet Lord Byron, she was raised with an unusually rigorous education in mathematics and logic, encouraged by her mother who hoped to steer her away from her father's poetic temperament.

In the 1840s she worked with Charles Babbage on his proposed mechanical general-purpose computer, the Analytical Engine. While translating an article about the machine, she appended a set of extensive notes of her own.

Those notes contained what is now recognised as the first published algorithm intended to be carried out by a machine, making her arguably the first computer programmer. She also foresaw that such machines could go beyond pure calculation to manipulate symbols and even compose music.

Her visionary ideas were far ahead of her time and were largely overlooked until the twentieth century, when her contributions were rediscovered and celebrated. The programming language Ada, developed for the US Department of Defense, is named in her honour.

Key Achievements

  • 1815 — Born in London, England.
  • 1833 — Meets Charles Babbage and his Difference Engine.
  • 1843 — Publishes her translation and notes on the Analytical Engine, including the first algorithm.
  • 1980 — The Ada programming language is named after her.
